Know exactly how much soil to buy and what to mix. This chart covers soil volume for every common raised bed size, 4 proven soil recipes, bulk-vs-bag costs, and the filling tips that save money and grow better plants.

1. The Volume Formula

Volume (cu ft) = Length (ft) × Width (ft) × (Depth (in) ÷ 12) × Number of Beds
Volume (cu yd) = Volume (cu ft) ÷ 27
Volume (gallons) = Volume (cu ft) × 7.48
Bags (2 cu ft) = Volume (cu ft) ÷ 2 (round up)

3. Four Proven Soil Mix Recipes

🥇 Balanced Mix (All-Purpose) — 60/30/10

Topsoil 60%
Compost 30%
Perlite 10%

Best for most vegetables. Topsoil gives structure, compost feeds plants, perlite improves drainage and aeration.

🥈 Mel's Mix (Square Foot Gardening) — 1/3 each

Topsoil 33%
Compost 33%
Peat 34%

Lightweight and nutrient-rich. Peat moss retains moisture but is not renewable — substitute coco coir for a sustainable option.

🌿 Organic Mix (Heavy Feeders) — 50/30/20

Compost 50%
Topsoil 30%
Coir 20%

High compost content for heavy-feeding crops (tomatoes, corn, squash). Coir is a sustainable peat alternative that holds moisture well.

☁️ Lightweight Mix (Containers & Wet Climates) — 40/30/30

Topsoil 40%
Compost 30%
Perlite 30%

Extra perlite for fast drainage. Use for rooftop beds, containers, or gardens in rainy climates where waterlogging is a risk.

7. Filling & Maintenance Tips

🚫 Never Use Garden Soil Alone

Garden soil compacts in raised beds, drains poorly, and may contain pests and weed seeds. Always mix with compost and a lightening agent (perlite, vermiculite, or coir).

🚛 Buy in Bulk for Big Projects

For more than ~20 cu ft, buy topsoil and compost by the cubic yard. One cubic yard fills about thirteen 2-cu-ft bags — at half the cost.

📏 Leave Headspace

Fill beds to within 1–2 inches of the rim. This leaves room for watering without runoff and space for a layer of mulch on top.

🔄 Mix Thoroughly

Blend all components together before filling, or layer and till. Avoid pockets of pure peat or perlite that throw off water and nutrient balance.

🌱 Top-Dress Annually

Soil settles and compost decomposes. Each spring, add 1–2 inches of compost to replenish nutrients and organic matter. No need to replace all soil.

🧱 Save on Fill

For deep beds, fill the bottom third with organic "browns" (straw, leaves, wood chips) to save soil. They decompose over time, adding organic matter.

💧 Water as You Fill

Moisten each layer as you build the bed. This settles the soil and prevents large air pockets that dry out roots.

🧪 Test Before Planting

Test pH and N-P-K of your finished mix. Adjust with lime, sulfur, or fertilizer based on what you're growing. Compost pH is usually near-neutral.
