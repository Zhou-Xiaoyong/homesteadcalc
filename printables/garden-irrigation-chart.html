💧 Garden Irrigation Chart

← Back to All Printables

Water the right amount, not more. This chart gives you weekly water needs by crop, climate multipliers, and irrigation efficiency factors so you can stop guessing and start watering with confidence.

1. The Core Formula

Water Needed (in/week) = Crop Factor × Climate Factor − Rainfall
Water Needed (gal/week) = Area (sq ft) × Water (in) × 0.623
Actual Water to Apply = Water Needed ÷ Irrigation Efficiency
(1 inch of water over 1 sq ft = 0.623 gallons)

3. Climate Adjustment Factors

ClimateMultiplierTypical RegionsEffect on Water Needs
Cool / Maritime0.7×Pacific NW, coastal NEReduces needs by ~30%
Temperate1.0×Midwest, mid-AtlanticBase (no adjustment)
Hot / Dry1.3×Southern plains, interior WestIncreases needs by ~30%
Desert / High Heat1.5×Southwest, deep South summerIncreases needs by ~50%

Hot, dry, and windy conditions increase evapotranspiration (water lost from soil + plant leaves). Use the multiplier that best matches your summer conditions.

4. Irrigation Method Efficiency

MethodEfficiencyWater LostBest For
Drip irrigation92%8%Raised beds, rows, containers — most efficient
Soaker hose75%25%Rows, beds; low-cost drip alternative
Hand watering (wand)70%30%Small gardens, containers
Sprinkler (above-ground)65%35%Large areas; loses most to wind & evap

Lower efficiency means you apply more water to deliver the same amount to the roots. Divide your needed gallons by the efficiency (e.g., 100 gal needed ÷ 0.65 = 154 gal to apply with a sprinkler).

6. Watering Best Practices

🌅 Water in the Morning

Early morning (5–8 AM) minimizes evaporation and gives foliage time to dry before evening, reducing fungal disease.

📏 Deep & Infrequent

Apply 1–2 inches at a time, 2–3 times per week. Shallow daily watering creates shallow, weak roots.

👆 Finger Test

Stick your finger 2 inches into the soil. If it's dry at that depth, it's time to water. If damp, wait.

🌧️ Track Rainfall

Use a rain gauge. Subtract weekly rainfall from your crop's water needs before irrigating — most gardens get 1+ in/week in summer.

🍃 Water the Soil, Not Leaves

Wet leaves invite fungal blight. Use drip, soaker hoses, or aim water at the base of plants.

🛏️ Mulch Heavily

2–3 inches of organic mulch (straw, wood chips) can cut watering needs by up to 50% by reducing evaporation.

🍅 Adjust by Stage

Seedlings need frequent light water. Fruiting crops need the most. Reduce water as crops near harvest to concentrate flavor.

🌡️ 1 Inch = 6–8 Inches Deep

One inch of water typically penetrates 6–8 inches into loam soil. Sandy soils need more frequent watering; clay soils less.
