2. Nutrient Availability Over Time

NutrientYear 1Year 2Year 3Notes
Nitrogen (N)50%25%10%Only half is available immediately; rest is slow-release organic N
Phosphorus (P₂O₅)80–90%~10%—Most P is available the first year; it binds to soil over time
Potassium (K₂O)80–90%~10%—Highly available; leaches more readily than P

Why this matters: If you apply manure every year, nitrogen accumulates. Account for residual N from previous years' applications to avoid over-fertilizing, which wastes money, burns plants, and pollutes waterways.

5. Manure Safety Rules for Food Crops

⚠️ Raw Manure & Pathogens

Fresh manure may contain E. coli, Salmonella, and other pathogens. Never apply raw manure to crops that will be eaten raw within the waiting periods below. Composting to 131°F (55°C) for 3 consecutive days kills most pathogens.

6. Best Uses by Manure Type

🐔 Chicken Manure

Highest N — great for heavy feeders (corn, tomatoes, squash). Must be composted or aged; fresh chicken manure burns plants. Compost first to reduce pathogens.

🐇 Rabbit Manure

"Cold" manure — safe to apply fresh directly to the garden. High N, won't burn. Pellet form makes it easy to side-dress plants. Excellent for raised beds.

🐑 Sheep / Goat Manure

Also a "cold" manure — safe fresh. Pelletized, easy to spread. Good balanced N-P-K. Excellent for vegetable gardens and can be applied any time.

🐎 Horse Manure

Often has lots of bedding (straw/shavings) which is high carbon. Compost well to avoid nitrogen tie-up. Great for building soil organic matter.

🐄 Cow Manure

Lower N, high organic matter. Mild and safe when composted. Great for conditioning soil and top-dressing. Less risk of burning than chicken manure.

🐖 Pig Manure

Similar to cow in N but higher P. Must be composted — pig manure can carry parasites. Good for fields and compost piles, less ideal for direct garden use.

7. Application Tips

🔬 Test Your Soil

Test N-P-K and pH before applying manure. Over-application of phosphorus (common with manure) can lock up micronutrients and pollute streams.

📅 Apply in Fall or Early Spring

Incorporate manure into the soil before planting. Fall applications give organic matter time to break down and nutrients to stabilize.

🌿 Compost First When Possible

Composting kills pathogens, reduces volume, stabilizes N, and eliminates the "burn" risk. Aged (6+ months) manure is the next best thing.

💧 Water It In

Always water manure into the soil to prevent nutrient runoff, volatilization (N loss as ammonia), and attraction of pests.

♻️ Account for Residual N

25% of last year's N and 10% from 2 years ago is available this year. Reduce fresh applications accordingly to avoid over-fertilizing.

🚫 Avoid Runoff

Don't apply manure before heavy rain. Keep it out of waterways and wells. Buffer strips of grass along streams help filter runoff.
