🧪 Soil pH Amendment Chart

← Back to All Printables

Get your soil pH right and nutrients unlock themselves. This chart shows how much lime or sulfur to add per pH point, the ideal pH for every common garden crop, and how to time your applications for best results.

1. The pH Scale at a Glance

3.5 – 6.0 Acidic (add lime)
6.0 – 7.0 Ideal
7.0 – 9.5 Alkaline (add sulfur)

Most vegetables thrive between pH 6.0 and 7.0 because that's where nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, and micronutrients are most available.

5. Amendment Timing & Application Tips

⏱️ Lime Needs Time

Lime takes 2–6 months to fully react with soil. Apply in fall for spring planting, or at least 2–3 months before you need the pH change.

🔄 Work It In

Spread lime or sulfur evenly and till it into the top 6–8 inches of soil. Surface applications work much slower.

💧 Water After Applying

Water the amendment in thoroughly right after applying to start the chemical reaction and prevent runoff.

🧪 Always Retest

Retest soil pH 3–6 months after amending. Don't guess — over-amending (especially sulfur) can swing pH too far the other way.

📏 Don't Over-Lime Sandy Soil

Sandy soils have low buffering capacity. A little lime goes a long way. Apply half the rate, retest, then add more if needed.

🌿 Organic Matter Buffers pH

Adding compost and organic matter helps stabilize pH over time and reduces the need for frequent amendments.
