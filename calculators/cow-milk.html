How It Works

A dairy cow's milk production depends mostly on breed and stage of lactation. Each breed has a typical peak daily yield and butterfat percentage. Production ramps up after calving, peaks around 4–8 weeks, then slowly declines until the cow is dried off 60 days before her next calf.

Daily Milk (gal) = Breed Peak (gal/day) × Lactation Stage Factor Annual Milk (gal) = Daily Milk × Milking Days × Number of Cows Annual Milk (lbs) = Annual Milk (gal) × 8.6 Butter (lbs) = Annual Milk (gal) × (Butterfat % ÷ 4.0) × 0.45 Cheese (lbs) = Annual Milk (gal) × 1.2 Yogurt (gal) = Annual Milk (gal) × 0.95 Cream (gal) = Annual Milk (gal) × 0.10 Annual Milk Income = Annual Milk (gal) × Milk Price Annual Feed Cost = Feed Cost × 365 × Number of Cows Net Income = Milk Income − Feed Cost Feed Cost per Gallon = Annual Feed Cost ÷ Annual Milk (gal)

Breed Production Differences

Holsteins are the milk volume champions — 8–12 gallons a day at peak — but their milk is lower in butterfat (3.5–4.0%). Jerseys give less milk (5–8 gallons) but it's much richer (4.5–5.5% fat), making them ideal for butter and cheese. Crossbreds and Brown Swiss offer a good balance of volume and components for a family cow.

The Lactation Curve

After a cow freshens (calves), milk production climbs to a peak at 4–8 weeks. From there, it drops about 2.5% per week. So a cow giving 10 gallons at peak might give 9 gallons at 4 months, 7 at 7 months, and 5–6 by the time she's dried off at 10 months. This calculator lets you pick the stage to estimate current production; annual figures assume the stage you select as an average.

Is a Family Cow Worth It?

A single family cow produces far more milk than a family can drink — 5–10 gallons a day. The value comes from turning surplus milk into butter, cheese, yogurt, and cream, or selling raw milk. If feed costs run $5/day and milk sells for $4/gallon, a cow producing 6 gallons/day covers her feed and leaves surplus dairy products for the family.
