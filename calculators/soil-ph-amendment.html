How It Works

Soil pH measures how acidic or alkaline your soil is on a 0–14 scale. Most vegetables grow best between pH 6.0 and 7.0 because that's where essential nutrients are most available. If your soil is too acidic, add lime (calcium carbonate). If it's too alkaline, add elemental sulfur.

pH Change = Target pH − Current pH If raising pH (lime needed): Lime (lbs) = Area ÷ 1000 × pH Change × Lime Rate Lime Rate per 1,000 sq ft per pH point: Sandy soil: 25 lbs Loam soil: 50 lbs Clay soil: 75 lbs If lowering pH (sulfur needed): Sulfur (lbs) = Area ÷ 1000 × pH Change × Sulfur Rate Sulfur Rate per 1,000 sq ft per pH point: Sandy soil: 5 lbs Loam soil: 10 lbs Clay soil: 15 lbs

Why Soil Type Matters

Clay soils have a high buffering capacity — they resist pH change because clay particles hold onto hydrogen ions. Sandy soils have low buffering, so small amendments cause large pH swings. That's why clay needs up to 3× more lime per pH point than sand.

Tips for Applying Amendments

Lime: Work into the top 6–8 inches of soil. It takes 2–6 months to fully react, so apply in fall for spring planting. Water it in well.

Sulfur: Elemental sulfur is converted to sulfuric acid by soil bacteria over weeks to months. Apply in advance of planting. Aluminum sulfate works faster but adds aluminum which can be toxic to some plants.

Retest: Always retest your soil pH 3–6 months after amending to verify the change.
