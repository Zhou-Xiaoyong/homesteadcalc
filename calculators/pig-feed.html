How It Works

Raising a pig for meat is one of the most efficient ways to produce pork on a homestead. The biggest expense by far is feed. This calculator estimates how much feed your pigs will eat, the total cost from piglet to freezer, and the true cost per pound of finished meat.

Weight Gain = Market Weight − Starting Weight Total Feed (per pig) = Weight Gain × Feed Conversion Ratio × (1 − Pasture Savings %) Feed Cost = Total Feed × Feed Cost per lb Total Cost = (Feed Cost + Piglet Cost + Processing Cost) × Number of Pigs Hanging Weight = Market Weight × 0.72 Retail Weight = Hanging Weight × 0.62 (≈ 45% of live weight) Cost per lb (live) = Total Cost ÷ (Market Weight × Number of Pigs) Cost per lb (retail) = Total Cost ÷ (Retail Weight × Number of Pigs)

Understanding Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR)

FCR is how many pounds of feed a pig needs to gain one pound of body weight. Modern commercial pigs convert at 2.5–2.8 lbs of feed per pound of gain. Homestead pigs that get pasture, scraps, or move around more typically run 3.0–3.5. Heritage breeds and older pigs can be 3.5–4.0. The lower the FCR, the more efficient the pig.

How Much Meat Do You Actually Get?

A 260-lb live pig yields roughly:

Hanging weight: ~187 lbs (72% of live) — this is what the butcher weighs after slaughter and gutting.

Retail cuts: ~116–120 lbs (62% of hanging, or ~45% of live) — boneless, wrapped, ready for the freezer. Bones, skin, and trim are removed (some of which can be used for stock or lard).

Tips for Reducing Cost

Pasture and forage: Good pasture, dairy byproducts, and kitchen scraps can cut feed costs by 10–25%. Pigs are excellent foragers.

Buy feed in bulk: A ton of bagged feed costs significantly more than the same feed bought in bulk. Many feed mills sell 50-lb bags or will deliver by the ton.

Don't overfinish: Past 280 lbs, pigs put on more fat than muscle and feed efficiency drops. Process at 250–280 lbs for the best cost-per-pound.

Raise more at once: Pigs compete less and grow better with company. Raising 2–4 pigs together spreads fixed costs (processing, setup) and lets you buy feed in larger quantities.
