How It Works

The volume of soil you need is simply the volume of your raised bed: length × width × depth. Convert depth from inches to feet by dividing by 12. The calculator then splits the total volume into the components of your chosen soil mix recipe.

Volume (cu ft) = Length (ft) × Width (ft) × (Depth (in) ÷ 12) × Number of Beds Volume (cu yd) = Volume (cu ft) ÷ 27 Volume (gallons) = Volume (cu ft) × 7.48 Each component = Total Volume × Component Percentage

Choosing the Right Soil Mix

Balanced (60/30/10): Great all-purpose mix for most vegetables. Topsoil provides structure, compost adds nutrients, perlite improves drainage.

Mel's Mix (1/3 each): Made famous by Square Foot Gardening. Lightweight and nutrient-rich. Peat moss retains moisture but is not renewable — consider coir as an alternative.

Organic (50/30/20): High compost content for heavy feeders. Coir is a sustainable peat alternative.

Lightweight (40/30/30): Extra perlite for containers and beds where drainage is critical (e.g., roofs, wet climates).

Tips

Buy in bulk: Soil is much cheaper by the cubic yard than by the bag. One cubic yard = 27 cu ft = about 13–14 two-cubic-foot bags.

Don't use garden soil alone: It compacts in raised beds and drains poorly. Always mix with compost and a lightening agent.

Leave 1–2 inches at the top: Fill beds to within 1–2 inches of the rim to leave room for watering and mulch.
