How Much Stuffing for a 20-Pound Turkey?

Published: November 10, 2026 · 8 min read

Fifteen cups of stuffing for a 20-pound bird. That is USDA's three-quarters of a cup per pound, and it comes out of 1 1/2 pounds of bread, five ribs of celery, two large onions, and one 13-by-9 pan.

Most lists stop at two loaves and a bunch of celery, which is why the pan overflows at ten in the morning. Count forward from the bird and three things settle at once: the bread, the dish, and whether the batch fits near the cavity.

Sixteen days out, this is a shopping decision. The bird is already in the freezer; the bread can wait until Monday.

Three-Quarters of a Cup per Pound

The figure comes from USDA's turkey guidance, written for a bird that gets stuffed: spoon it in loosely, about 3/4 cup per pound. Twelve pounds takes 9 cups. Twenty takes 15. Twenty-four takes 18, where ovens and roasting pans start arguing with you.

The cups column assumes a dozen guests, so a 24-pound bird at that table is a heavy plate. That is the normal outcome.

One and a Half Pounds of Bread

A pound of fresh bread cut into half-inch cubes gives about 10 cups. Drying drops the weight by a quarter and leaves the volume, so measure before drying. Fifteen cups is 1 1/2 pounds: one 24-ounce bakery loaf, or a pound loaf plus half its neighbor.

That is where the two-loaf habit comes from. Two pound loaves make 20 cups, a third more than a 20-pound bird needs, and the surplus is the second pan nobody planned. The garden-share math counted two loaves at $4 for this same table. One and a half is the honest number.

Stale is not a compromise

Fresh bread turns to paste. The starch in a new loaf is still gelatinized and collapses when the stock hits it, which is why the first batch of the season comes out dense. Cubes dried at 300 degrees Fahrenheit for 30 minutes hold their shape and soak up roughly their own weight in liquid.

Bake it yourself and the arithmetic simplifies. One 500-gram loaf on the Small setting is a pound and a half out of the oven, 15 to 16 cups, enough for a 20-pound bird with a cup for croutons.

The Cavity Takes Half the Batch

Here is what the rule does not say. Three-quarters of a cup per pound sizes a batch, not a cavity. A 20-pound bird swallows 6 to 8 cups spooned in loose, and the neck flap another cup.

Check your own bird. Stand it in the sink, pour water into the cavity from a quart measure, and count. Empty it, dry it, and the number is yours to the cup.

Two things follow. The pan needs to hold 7 to 9 cups, and the stuffing inside the bird seasons the meat rather than feeding twelve.

Which Pan Holds Fifteen Cups

Pan volumes are arithmetic. A 13-by-9 two inches deep holds 16 cups to the rim, but stuffing rises close to an inch as the eggs and stock set, so the usable figure is 10. Fill it to the rim and it goes over at minute twenty.

A 3-inch-deep 13-by-9 solves this in one dish. The 2-inch pan works when half the batch goes into the bird, which is what most kitchens run.

Onion, Celery, and Butter by the Cup

Ratios hold across batch sizes, which is what makes 15 cups possible without a recipe. One cup each of diced onion and celery per 6 cups of bread. A quarter cup of stock per cup of cubes. Half a cup of butter per 12. Two eggs for binding past 10.

Celery is the ingredient worth growing here and the one most people fail at: 100 days, constant moisture, a cool finish. Five ribs is a 6-foot row that eats most of the season. The Garden Yield Planner will price it against the 99-cent bunch, which wins for one meal.

One Hundred Sixty-Five Degrees

Stuffing is wet, dense, and warm, which is the description of an incubator. The safe minimum is 165 degrees Fahrenheit in the center, measured with a thermometer rather than a clock. A bird can hit 165 in the thigh while the middle of the stuffing sits at 140.

Three habits cover the risk. Mix wet and dry separately and chill them, then combine right before stuffing. Never stuff a bird the night before. A stuffed bird goes straight into an oven at 325 or above.

Leftovers get two hours at room temperature, then shallow containers and the refrigerator, where they keep 3 to 4 days. Uncooked stuffing that is already mixed should be frozen, not refrigerated; cold slows bacteria without stopping them.

Fifty Minutes of Oven You Do Not Have

Pan dressing needs 35 minutes covered at 350 and 15 more uncovered. That is 50 minutes of an oven holding a bird, the same window the rolls want for 18 minutes at 375.

Three ways out. Bake the dressing at noon and hold it covered in a warm oven. Use the slow cooker, 45 minutes on high then 4 to 8 hours on low, the method University of Arkansas Extension publishes, which still has to read 165 in the center. Or eat later: the bird rests 20 to 30 minutes and carving takes 15 more.

Stuffing the bird costs 15 to 30 minutes of oven time too. A 20- to 24-pound bird runs 4 3/4 to 5 1/4 hours stuffed against 4 1/2 to 5 unstuffed, so the cavity is not the fast option. The rolls fit in the rest window; this does not.

What Fifteen Cups Cost

From scratch the batch runs $5 to $6: flour and yeast under a dollar, butter and two eggs near $1.50, celery and onions near $2.20, stock a dollar. Bagged cubes take a bag and a half for 15 cups, which puts the same dish at $12 to $14 before vegetables.

The flour is the interesting line. Five hundred grams is 1.1 pounds, and wheat to flour runs about 1.1 to 1, so the loaf is 1.2 pounds of wheat. At 50 to 60 pounds per 1,000 square feet that is 22 square feet, a bed 3 feet wide and 7 long. Grow the sage. Buy the flour.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much stuffing per person for Thanksgiving?

One cup a person when stuffing is one of six sides, a cup and a quarter when it is the side people fight over. The USDA rule runs the other way, three-quarters of a cup per pound, which puts twelve people at a 20-pound bird on 15 cups.

How many cups of bread cubes are in a loaf of bread?

About 10 cups of half-inch cubes per pound of fresh bread. A 24-ounce bakery loaf gives 15 cups, exactly what a 20-pound bird calls for. A 500-gram loaf baked at home comes out near a pound and a half, which lands in the same place at 15 to 16 cups.

Can I stuff the turkey the night before?

No. Mix the wet and dry parts separately and chill them, then combine just before the stuffing goes into the bird. Once mixed, uncooked stuffing should be frozen rather than refrigerated, and a stuffed bird goes straight into an oven set no lower than 325 degrees Fahrenheit.

Does stuffing a turkey take longer to cook?

Fifteen to thirty minutes longer at 325 degrees Fahrenheit. A 20- to 24-pound bird runs 4 3/4 to 5 1/4 hours stuffed against 4 1/2 to 5 unstuffed, and it is not done until the stuffing reads 165 degrees Fahrenheit, even if the thigh got there first.

The Numbers to Write Down

Three-quarters of a cup per pound, 15 cups for a 20-pound bird, 1 1/2 pounds of bread at 10 cups a pound, 8 cups in the cavity and 7 in a 13-by-9, 165 degrees in the center. Buy one 24-ounce loaf and five ribs of celery.

The exception is a family that treats stuffing as the main event. Plan a cup and a half a person, skip the cavity, and bake two 3-inch-deep pans so the crisp top is not one square foot fought over at the end of the line.

For the rest of the menu, the garden share of a twelve-person dinner covers the vegetables and the freezer plan covers the bird. Baking the bread yourself? Set the flour weight first.
