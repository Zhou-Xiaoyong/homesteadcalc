How Much Does It Cost to Raise a Turkey?

Published: November 15, 2026 · 9 min read

Forty-one dollars a bird, or $2.73 a pound dressed. That covers a poult, 55 pounds of feed, and a bag of shavings for a hen that finishes at 20 pounds live and hangs at 15.

The same turkey gets quoted at $39 and at $79 in the same week, both from the same arithmetic. Two lines swing it: who does the killing and plucking, and how many birds share one heat lamp. Everything below is per bird at 2026 feed-store prices.

Feed Is Two-Thirds of the Bill

A turkey converts 2.6 to 3.0 pounds of feed into a pound of live weight, which is worse than a Cornish Cross broiler at 1.8 to 2.0 and better than a goat. Land on 20 pounds live and the bird has eaten 52 to 59 pounds of feed. Plan for 55.

That feed is not one product. Protein drops in three steps as the bird grows, and the starter costs the most per pound because it carries 28 percent protein and a coccidiostat.

Phase Weeks Protein Feed per bird Feed per 4 birds
Starter 0 to 6 28% 13 lb 52 lb (1 bag)
Grower 7 to 12 20% 19 lb 76 lb (use 2 bags)
Finisher 13 to 18 18% 23 lb 92 lb (use 2 bags)

Five 50-pound bags cover four birds: $26 for the starter, $23 each for the other four, so $118 at the register. Four birds eat 220 pounds of that 250, and the 30 left in the bin go to the chickens. Priced as eaten, 55 pounds comes to $26 a bird. Buy three bags at a time for one bird and the blended price climbs to $0.54, which is where the $32 feed bill in the garden-share arithmetic comes from. Same feed, different quantity discount.

Watch the last six weeks: two-fifths of the feed goes in during the final month, when a finishing hen eats about half a pound a day. Run out of finisher in week 16 and you will be buying layer pellets at retail to finish a bird that is already 80 percent grown.

What a Pound of It Costs, Four Ways

The same 20-pound live bird, priced four ways, next to what the store charges. Dressed weight is 15 pounds at 75 percent, cooked meat 6.8 pounds at 45 percent, a plate six ounces.

Version Per bird Per lb dressed Per 6-oz plate
Feed, poult, bedding only $41 $2.73 $2.28
Plus brooder power $52 $3.47 $2.89
Plus processing, 4-bird batch $59 $3.93 $3.28
Single bird, processor minimum $79 $5.27 $4.39
Commodity frozen, 20 lb $26 $1.29 $1.08
Heritage retail, 20 lb $180 $9.00 $8.45

The top row is what most people mean by the cost of raising a turkey, and it is honest as far as it goes. It stops before the lamp and the plucker.

What the Brooder Lamp Actually Costs

Poults need 95 degrees Fahrenheit at floor level in week one, dropped about 5 degrees a week until they are feathered out at six weeks. A 250-watt heat lamp running around the clock for those six weeks pulls 252 kilowatt-hours. At the 2026 residential average of $0.17, that is $43 of electricity for the batch, or $10.75 a bird when four share the brooder.

Nobody puts that on a cost sheet, and it is 42 percent of what the feed costs. Two ways to cut it: a 150-watt ceramic bulb on a thermostat, and moving the birds off heat at five weeks instead of six if the weather holds. Both are worth about $10 a season on a four-bird batch.

Water matters less than people expect. A turkey drinks 1.5 to 2 times its feed weight, so four birds go through 40 to 50 gallons over the season. That is a rounding error unless you are hauling it.

Processing, or the $28 Minimum

A licensed processor with a minimum charge bills $28 whether you hand over one bird or four. Spread across a four-bird batch that is $7 a bird. Bring a single turkey and you pay the whole minimum, which is the entire gap between the $59 row and the $79 row in the table above.

Doing it yourself costs nothing but an afternoon and a sharp knife. The four-hour processing timeline covers the scald, the pluck, and the ice bath. The bird then needs to hang and chill, then rest a day or two in the fridge before it goes in the oven, because rigor has to pass or the breast cooks tough. That rest is not optional and it does not fit in a 24-hour plan.

One more line people forget: a hen dresses at 72 to 75 percent of live weight, a tom at 76 to 78. If you are buying poults by the pound of expected dressed weight, buy toms.

The 18-Week Countdown

This is the part that catches people in November. You cannot start a turkey in the fall and eat it at Thanksgiving. Working backward from Thursday, November 26, 2026:

When What happens
January Order poults. Broad-breasted varieties sell out by March.
July 9 (toms) or July 23 (hens) Poults arrive. Brooder at 95°F, 2 sq ft per bird.
August to September Grower feed, range access at 10 sq ft per bird.
Late September Switch to finisher. This is the expensive month.
November 20 to 22 Process, chill, and rest in the fridge.
November 26 Roast. A 15-pound dressed bird takes about 3 1/2 hours at 325°F.

Hatcheries ship 15 or 25 poults, not four. That is the real barrier for a household wanting two birds: split an order with neighbors, buy from a farm store in spring, or raise the rest and sell them. Eight sold at $4 a pound dressed covers the feed for the four you keep.

Heritage Birds Cost More on Purpose

A Bourbon Red or Narragansett converts closer to 3.5 pounds of feed per pound of gain and needs 26 to 28 weeks, not 18. Feed goes to 70 pounds, the poult costs $14 instead of $10, and the dressed bird is smaller at the same age. That is why heritage retail runs $9 a pound and why the bird on your table has a narrower breast and more dark meat.

What the extra $35 buys: a bird that forages and mates on its own, so you hatch next year's flock instead of ordering poults. The self-sufficiency score counts a breeding trio as closed-loop where bought poults are not.

Whether It Pays

It does not pay against the freezer case. Fifteen pounds dressed at home runs $41 to $59 against $26 for a commodity bird, and the plate cost is $2.28 to $3.28 against $1.08. Anyone telling you home-raised turkey saves money is comparing it to heritage retail at $9 a pound, which is a fair comparison and a narrow one.

What the money buys is an 18-week-old bird instead of a 14-week-old one, feed you selected, and a carcass that never saw a chlorine chill tank. If that is worth $33 a year to you, raise two. If it is not, buy the frozen bird and put the difference into the garden, where the returns are real: 38 square feet of vegetables replaces $33 of produce for $5 of seed.

The pounds on the table and the salt in the brine both start from dressed weight. Sizing the bird and brining a 20-pounder pick up where this leaves off, and the freezer-space math says whether 15 pounds fits.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to raise one turkey?

About $41 for a bird that dresses at 15 pounds: $10 for the poult, $26 of feed, and $5 of shavings. That is $2.73 a pound dressed. Power for the brooder lamp adds $11 and paid processing adds $7 to $28 depending on whether the processor charges you by the bird or by the trip.

How much feed does a turkey eat to reach 20 pounds?

Fifty-two to 59 pounds, because a turkey converts 2.6 to 3.0 pounds of feed into a pound of live weight. A third of it goes in during the last six weeks, when a finishing hen cleans up about half a pound a day and a tom eats more.

Is it cheaper to raise your own turkey than to buy one?

No. A commodity frozen bird is $1.29 a pound and a home-raised one is $2.73 to $3.47, so a 15-pound dressed turkey costs $41 to $52 at home against $26 at the store. The money buys a bird raised outdoors for 18 weeks on feed you chose, not a lower grocery bill.

When do you have to start poults for Thanksgiving?

Late July. Hens need 18 weeks and toms 20 to reach 20 pounds live, which puts hatch day on July 23 or July 9 for a November 26 holiday. Order in January, because hatcheries sell out of the broad-breasted varieties by March and ship poults in lots of 15 or 25.

The Numbers to Write Down

Fifty-five pounds of feed, one $10 poult, five bags for four birds, and $43 of brooder power. That is $52 a bird and $3.47 a pound dressed, or $2.89 a plate, against $1.08 from the freezer case.

The exception is a batch you sell into. Eight birds sold at $4 a pound dressed covers the feed for the four you keep, and that is the only version where the arithmetic favors the backyard.
