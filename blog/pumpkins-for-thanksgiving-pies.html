How Many Pumpkins Does It Take to Make Thanksgiving Pies?

Published: November 7, 2026 · 8 min read

Twelve people eat about three 9-inch pumpkin pies, and that takes three 5-pound sugar pumpkins. The whole chain runs on one number: half a cup of drained puree per pound of whole pumpkin. A 5-pounder gives 2 1/2 cups. A pie takes 1 3/4.

From the pantry side it is shorter. One 15-ounce can holds 1 3/4 cups, which is one pie, which is why the can and the pie tin have matched each other for fifty years.

Nineteen days out, the question is how many to buy.

One Pumpkin, One and a Half Pies

Work from the pan backwards. A standard 9-inch plate takes 1 3/4 cups of filling. A deep-dish plate takes 2 1/4.

Whole pumpkin converts at roughly half a cup per pound, after roasting and draining. A 5-pound sugar pumpkin is about 3 pounds of flesh once the stem, seeds, and rind come off, and roasting drives off enough water that they measure near 2 1/2 cups.

4 lb 2 1/2 lb 2 cups 1
5 lb 3 lb 2 1/2 cups 1, plus a cup over
6 lb 3 3/4 lb 3 cups 1 3/4
8 lb 5 lb 4 cups 2 1/4

The rule of thumb that a 5-pound pumpkin makes two pies is generous by half. Two pies need 3 1/2 cups, so you would need a 7-pounder. Thick-rinded pumpkins run closer to a third of a cup per pound, which is why one extra pumpkin costs $4 and saves a second trip.

Why Canned Pumpkin Is a Different Vegetable

Libby's grows a Dickinson squash for its cans and labels it pumpkin, which is legal and also a hint about what makes a good pie. Dickinson is a Cucurbita moschata, the same species as butternut, bred for dense orange flesh rather than for a face.

The pumpkin in a carving patch is usually Howden or Connecticut Field, 15 to 30 pounds, selected over a century for size and a strong handle. Twice as watery, and stringier. That extra water dilutes the sugar and the starch, so a field pumpkin custard weeps onto the crust or stays loose in the middle while the edge overcooks.

Pie varieties are a different list: Small Sugar at 6 to 7 pounds and 100 days, Baby Pam at 4 to 6, and Long Pie, a banana-shaped heirloom that stores into spring.

How Many Pies for Twelve People

Eight slices to a 9-inch pie, and the average guest takes one and a third. Some pass, a few come back, and one uncle takes the largest piece. Add four spare slices for that and the formula is guests times 1.3, plus 4, divided by 8.

8 14 2 3 1/2 cups 2 1
12 20 3 5 1/4 cups 3 2
16 25 4 7 cups 3 2
20 30 4 7 cups 3 2
24 35 5 8 3/4 cups 4 2

Deep dish pans add a quarter cup of puree per pie. A pecan pie on the table cuts the pumpkin count by a pie, because half the room orders pecan.

Drain the Puree or the Pie Will Weep

Halve the pumpkins, scoop the seeds, and roast cut side down at 375 degrees Fahrenheit for 45 to 60 minutes, until a knife slides through the rind. Roasting concentrates the solids; boiling puts water back in.

Scoop, process, then hang in butter muslin in the refrigerator for two to four hours. Expect to lose a quarter to a half cup out of every 2 1/2. Finished puree should mound on a spoon and hold, not slump to the edges.

Pull the pie at 170 degrees Fahrenheit internal, not at a clean knife. Custard sets between 170 and 180, and carryover finishes it on the rack. A center that wobbles like set gelatin is correct. One that does not move hit 190, and that is the cracking on diner pies.

Freeze the surplus in 1 3/4-cup portions, one pie per bag, a year at 0 degrees. Canning is the wrong tool. The National Center for Home Food Preservation publishes tested times for cubed pumpkin, 55 minutes for pints and 90 for quarts at 10 pounds, and none for puree, because density keeps heat from reaching the center of a jar.

What Three Pies Cost Three Ways

Canned is the cheap route, and it is not close. That surprises people who assume the garden always wins on price.

Buying pie pumpkins in November is the most expensive route, at two to three times the canned price. Growing them is the cheapest by a factor of five. Either way the pie is a $9 to $30 line item, so the savings do not pay for a rototiller. Both pumpkin routes leave a cup or two over.

Grow them for flavor, then. Fresh puree tastes brighter and less sweet, and you can leave the sugar at half a cup instead of the full cup most recipes call for.

Two Vines, and Where They Go

Budget two useable pumpkins per vine. A healthy vine sets two to five fruit, but a wet June aborts flowers, so two is the number that holds in a bad year. Two vines carry a 12-person Thanksgiving, and the Garden Yield Planner turns the same per-plant counts into pounds.

In open ground, set vines 48 to 72 inches apart in rows 6 to 8 feet apart, or 24 to 48 square feet per plant. The Plant Spacing Calculator has pumpkin at 48 to 72 inches.

A cattle panel does the work of 40 square feet

Small pie pumpkins climb. Wire a 16-foot cattle panel to T-posts at the bed edge, set vines 30 inches apart along the base, and six plants fit in the footprint of two. Slip a strip of old T-shirt under each fruit at softball size, because a 6-pound pumpkin will pull its own stem off the vine.

Planning charts that list pumpkin at 9 square feet per plant assume this trellis. Open ground runs to 40. The cattle panel comparison covers gauge and footing.

Timing is the part you cannot fix this year. Small Sugar needs 100 days from transplant, so next year's seed goes in around June 1 in zone 5 and 6.

Buy Now, Cook on the 25th

Farm stand pumpkins in early November are cured, meaning the rind has hardened and the stem dried. That is what lets them sit two months instead of rotting in three weeks.

Keep them at 50 to 55 degrees Fahrenheit and 50 to 60 percent humidity, on a shelf or a bed of straw rather than bare concrete, and they hold two to four months. A pumpkin bought this week is still good in January. Do not wash it. Wipe with a dry cloth, carry it from underneath, and leave three to four inches of stem on.

Check the shelf weekly and cook anything soft, because one going off takes the ones touching it. The Vegetable Storage Calculator gives the same targets for squash, potatoes, onions, and seven other crops, plus the pounds lost over a winter.

Wait for the 24th and you pick from a bare bin at the same price.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many pumpkins do I need for three pumpkin pies?

Three five-pound sugar pumpkins, which give about 7 1/2 cups of drained puree against the 5 1/4 cups three pies actually need. Two pumpkins will do if they run six to eight pounds each. Buy one spare, because a thick-rinded pumpkin yields closer to a third of a cup per pound.

Can I use a carving pumpkin for pumpkin pie?

You can, and it will taste flat and watery. Field pumpkins bred for carving run 15 to 30 pounds with about twice the water of a pie type, so the puree needs a longer drain and an extra egg to set. Sugar pie varieties such as Small Sugar, Baby Pam, and Long Pie weigh 4 to 8 pounds and carry roughly double the solids.

How much puree is in a 15-ounce can of pumpkin?

One and three-quarter cups, which is exactly what a standard 9-inch pie takes. A 29-ounce can holds 3 1/2 cups, a little under double, and covers two pies. Both sizes are already drained, so there is no straining step.

How long does homemade pumpkin puree keep?

Four days in the refrigerator, or a year at 0 degrees Fahrenheit in half-cup portions. Freeze it in 1 3/4-cup bags so each one is a pie. Do not water bath or pressure can the puree: the National Center for Home Food Preservation publishes tested times for cubed pumpkin only, because mashed puree is too dense for heat to reach the center.

The Numbers to Write Down

Half a cup of drained puree per pound of whole pumpkin. One and three-quarter cups per 9-inch pie. One and a third slices per guest, plus four. Two useable pumpkins per vine.

Those four numbers cover a Thanksgiving from seed order to last slice. The exception is a household that serves pie for breakfast on the 27th. Double the count.
