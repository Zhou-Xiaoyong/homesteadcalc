Chicken Coop Size for 10 Chickens: Dimensions That Work

Published: October 20, 2026 · 9 min read

Ten standard-size hens need 40 square feet of coop floor and 100 square feet of run: a 5×8 or 6×7 coop against a 10×10 run, with 8.3 feet of roost bar and two nest boxes. Those four numbers decide whether ten birds are pleasant or a pecking-order mess by March.

Floor space is where most coops fail. A unit sold as fitting 8 to 10 chickens usually carries 12 to 16 square feet of floor, because the catalog footprint counts the nest box and the feed locker. Ten hens in a 4×6 box means bare backs, dirty eggs, and ammonia you can smell from the driveway.

Penn State Extension puts standard breeds at 2 to 3 square feet per bird indoors and 8 to 10 in the run, with 4 for heavy breeds. The Chicken Coop Size Calculator uses the top of that range, so ten birds come back as 40 square feet. Build to it if the flock spends January indoors.

The Numbers for Ten Hens

These assume standard dual-purpose birds, 6 to 8 lb, shut in at night.

Requirement For 10 standard hens Rule behind it
Coop floor 40 sq ft 4 sq ft per bird; 30 sq ft is the hard floor
Buildable size 5×8 ft (40 sq ft) 6×8 gives 48 sq ft and 1.5 sheets of plywood
Run area 100 sq ft 10 sq ft per bird; 50 if they free-range all day
Roost length 100 in (8.3 ft) 10 in per bird, wide face of a 2×4 up
Nest boxes 2, three is better 1 per 4 to 5 hens, 12×12 in each
Ventilation 4 sq ft of opening 1 sq ft per 10 sq ft of floor, above head height
Feeder space 40 in of trough 4 in per bird, so all ten eat at once

Forty inches of trough sounds generous until ten hens crowd a 24-inch feeder. The bottom two in the order get pushed off and eat after dark, which is how feather picking starts.

Turning 40 Square Feet Into a Floor Plan

Sheathing comes in 4×8 sheets, so plans that divide into 4-foot increments waste less plywood and less time.

Floor plan Area Sq ft per bird 4×8 sheets Verdict

6×8 ft 48 sq ft 4.8 1.5 Best balance for ten birds

Why 6×8 usually wins

Ten inches per bird is 8.3 feet of bar. A 5×8 wall is 5 feet, so every bird sleeps in two tiers whether the design wanted it or not. A 6×8 gives two 4-foot bars for 8 feet total and costs one more sheet.

Headroom

Four feet at the low side and six at the high side is enough for ten hens. Not for you. A 4-foot coop means kneeling in the pop door with a scraper, four times a year minimum. Six feet of headroom runs about $120 more in framing and a real door, cheap now and painful to retrofit.

Floor space versus footprint

The calculator adds 16 square feet for feed storage and another 16 for a brooder, and both are honest. A 2-foot-deep locker on the end wall of a 6×8 holds four 50-lb bags and keeps rodents out of the house. A brooder needs its own wire lid, not a corner the adults can reach.

Run Size: 100 Square Feet

A 10×10 square at six feet tall lets you walk in with a bucket. Ten hens on bare dirt strip it to mud in about three weeks.

When 50 square feet is enough

Flocks that range over grass from dawn to dusk get 50, which is what the calculator returns for free-range housing. Free-ranging is a daytime arrangement. The run still has to exist for hawk days and two feet of snow.

Roof half of it

An open run with ten hens turns to standing water by the second wet week. Metal over 50 square feet keeps a dry patch and keeps feeders out of the weather. Budget $180 to $260 for panels, purlins, and posts.

Hardware cloth, not chicken wire

Half-inch cloth, four feet tall, with an 18-inch apron laid flat on the ground and pinned. Raccoons reach through one-inch mesh and they dig. Chicken wire keeps chickens in and nothing out.

Roost, Nest Boxes, and Ventilation

Two bars, not one

Ten hens want 100 inches of roost. Build a 4-foot bar at 20 inches off the floor and a second 4-foot bar at 32, offset 12 inches back so the upper birds clear the lower ones. Use 2×4s with the wide face up, keep 12 inches off the back wall, and hang a plywood dropping board 18 inches below with a front lip.

Three boxes beat two

One box per four to five hens puts ten birds at two, and hens will queue without complaint. Three costs about $20 more and covers the broody hen who claims a box for three weeks. Size them 12×12×12, 18 to 24 inches off the floor, lower than the roost, with a 4-inch lip.

Four square feet of vent

One square foot of opening per ten square feet of floor. Put it high, under the eaves, never at roost height. Ten hens fog the windows by morning, and a damp coop at 20°F is how frostbite gets into combs. If you smell ammonia at the door, the vent is undersized or the litter is due.

When Ten Hens Need 50 Square Feet Instead

Four square feet per bird is a working number for standard breeds in a mild climate. Four things push it to five, or 50 square feet overall.

  • Heavy breeds. Orpingtons, Brahmas, and Jersey Giants run 8 to 10 lb. At 5 square feet each, ten birds need 50 and the roost goes to 120 inches.
  • Cold winters. In Zone 4 and colder, birds stay inside for days during storms, and extra floor stops them trampling each other.
  • Storage and a brooder. Each adds 16 square feet of building, not of bird space.
  • Plans to grow. Ten hens become fourteen after one hatch. An 8×8 absorbs that without a second build.

Feeding and Watering Ten Hens

Ten layers eat about 2.5 lb of feed a day, so a 50-lb bag lasts roughly 20 days. Water runs 1.25 gallons a day in mild weather and closer to 2.5 at 90°F, which makes a 5-gallon waterer a four-day fill in April and a two-day fill in August.

Frequently Asked Questions

How big should a chicken coop be for 10 chickens?

Forty square feet of floor and 100 square feet of run for ten standard-size hens. That is a 5 by 8 or 6 by 7 coop with a 10 by 10 run, plus 8.3 feet of roost and two to three nest boxes. Heavy breeds at 8 to 10 pounds need closer to 50 square feet.

Will 10 chickens fit in an 8 by 8 coop?

Easily. An 8 by 8 gives 64 square feet, or 6.4 per bird at ten hens, and would hold 14 to 16 standard birds at 4 square feet each. Build it if the flock will grow; otherwise spend the difference on the run.

How many nesting boxes do 10 hens need?

Two at one box per four to five hens, and three ends the morning queue. Boxes should be 12 by 12 by 12 inches, 18 to 24 inches off the floor, and lower than the roost so birds do not sleep in them.

How much roost space do 10 chickens need?

Eighty to 100 linear inches, so 7 to 8.3 feet. A 6-foot-wide coop only holds a 72-inch bar, which is why most 10-bird coops need two bars stepped 12 inches apart in height and offset so droppings miss the birds below.

The Coop to Build

Frame a 6×8 with six feet of headroom: 48 square feet of floor, two 4-foot roosts stepped and offset, three nest boxes, four square feet of vent under the eaves. Wrap a 10×10 run in half-inch hardware cloth with an 18-inch apron, and roof half of it. Materials land at $1,200 to $1,700.

Forty square feet meets the standard. Forty-eight is what leaves room for a feed bag, a broody hen, and a week of bad weather. The exception is heavy breeds or Zone 4 winters, where ten birds want 50 square feet and an 8×8 footprint.
