Four Extra Guests for Thanksgiving? What to Add on the Day

Published: November 26, 2026 · 8 min read

Four extra plates need 3 more pounds of potatoes, a dozen more rolls, 4 more cups of stuffing, and one more pie. They do not need a second turkey, because a 20-pound bird already carries 16 people at 9 ounces of meat each.

That holds as long as the plates stay at 9 ounces. Keep the 12-ounce portions and the shortfall is 3 pounds of cooked meat, a 7-pound bird's worth, with no free oven to cook it in. Everything you add today comes from the stove, the counter, and the gravy boat.

What Four More Plates Add

The rates below match every other Thanksgiving calculation on this site, so the numbers line up with the list you already made. Twelve was the headcount. Sixteen is the new one.

The potato line is the one to act on first. Three pounds of russets is four or five potatoes, twenty minutes of peeling, thirty-five minutes in salted water, and a stick and a half of butter. What you do not cook keeps for months in a 40°F cellar.

Rolls come second, since the basket is what the table reaches for when the meat looks thin. A dozen more is one pound of flour, three and a half cups, and one batch of dough.

The 20-Pound Bird Already Feeds Sixteen

A dressed turkey yields about 45 percent of its weight as cooked meat. Twenty pounds gives 9 pounds, or 144 ounces. Divide by 12 and each plate gets 12 ounces. Divide by 16 and each gets 9.

Nine ounces is a restaurant portion. Twelve is a steakhouse portion. Nobody at a holiday table counts, but everybody notices a plate with one slice on it, and that is the failure to avoid.

Carve accordingly. Cut across the grain at a quarter inch and overlap the slices instead of stacking two half-inch slabs. Nine ounces fanned across the plate reads as more than twelve in a brick, and it holds gravy better. The weight-to-plate arithmetic is in the pounds per person guide.

Seconds are where this breaks. Four people at 12 ounces is 48 ounces cooked, or 6.7 pounds raw, and that is not fixable in an oven you do not have.

The Oven Is Booked From Eleven to Three-Thirty

A 20-pound unstuffed bird at 325°F takes four and a half to five hours. In at eleven, out at three-thirty, rest twenty minutes, carve at four. That cavity is full.

The obvious purchase, a 7-pound bone-in breast, wants two to two and a half hours at the same 325. Put both in and neither finishes on time: every opened door drops the cavity 25 to 50 degrees, and a roast that shared space runs twenty to forty minutes long.

Which leaves the counter appliances, the stovetop, the grill, and the store. A roaster holds a 20-pounder on its own circuit. A 6-quart slow cooker takes three pounds of meat in four hours on high. A sheet pan of chicken thighs needs forty minutes at 425, so it goes in after the bird comes out and while the gravy is reducing.

Scale the roll dough, not the pan

Pick dinner rolls and it returns flour, water, salt, and yeast in grams plus the bulk and proof times. The setting counts in four-ounce units, so a dozen rolls at 62 grams each goes in as six, and the output reads 744 grams of dough.

Proteins That Never Touch the Oven

Everything in this table can be on the table by four o'clock without displacing the bird. The portions assume it is a supplement to turkey, not a replacement.

Ham is the strongest buy this morning. A fully cooked ham takes ten minutes a pound to warm, and it can also be sliced cold at room temperature, which frees the oven entirely. Eight pounds is twelve portions.

Kielbasa is the homestead answer, since it is usually already in the freezer and three pounds browns in twenty minutes without anyone calling it a second main course.

Gravy and Bread Carry the Plate

Volume on a holiday plate is cheaper than meat by an order of magnitude. One cup of gravy is two tablespoons of fat, two tablespoons of flour, and a cup of stock. The extra one and a third cups is three tablespoons each, five minutes, and it covers eight more plates.

Do not thicken it to paste. A gravy that pours is what makes 9 ounces of turkey and a cup of mash look like dinner. If the drippings are short, yesterday's giblet stock covers it, and the gravy math covers the jarred backup.

Bread is the other lever. A loaf is three cups of flour and two hours with one rise, and it changes how a meal reads more than another pound of anything. Roll dough makes a loaf, which is what the dinner roll setting is for.

Cranberry sauce takes ten minutes from a bag of frozen berries and a cup of sugar. One 12-ounce bag yields two and a half cups, so it covers the extra cup twice over. The per-guest count is a quarter cup.

The Store Run, If You Make One

Call before you drive. On Thanksgiving Day the chain groceries are closed, and the independents and convenience stores that open tend to shut by two or three. A twenty-minute call saves a forty-minute round trip to a locked lot.

What is reliably left at noon: rotisserie chickens, the deli case, frozen pies, bags of brown-and-serve rolls, canned gravy, and bags of potatoes. Three chickens are twelve portions at five to seven dollars each, and pulling them is a ten-minute job at the counter.

What to skip: a frozen turkey, since cold water at 30 minutes a pound is a ten-hour bath; a fresh bird over four pounds, which needs two hours of oven; and anything from the deli case that will sit out past two hours. Buy sides that reheat, and take them to 165°F.

If It Is Already Three O'Clock

Then the arithmetic decides for you, and it is not a disaster. Sixteen at 9 ounces with four sides, good gravy, and warm bread is a normal Thanksgiving dinner. Sixteen at 12 ounces is not possible with one bird, and chasing it means carving to the bone.

So: slice thin, fan it wide, put the bread out early, double the gravy, and add a soup course from the giblet stock while the bird rests. Soup is fifteen minutes and it fills the gap between sitting down and the first plate, which is when sixteen people feel hungry.

With nothing in the house to add, a board of cheese, apples, pickles, olives, and crackers takes ten minutes and buys half an hour. On a plate that already has potatoes, stuffing, and gravy, most people take one pass at the meat and two at the sides. Feed the sides.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much more food do I need for four extra guests at Thanksgiving?

Three more pounds of potatoes, a dozen more rolls, four more cups of stuffing, one and a third cups of gravy, one cup of cranberry sauce, and one pie. That is about twenty more dollars at a farm stand and nothing at all from the freezer. The meat is the one item you probably do not add, because a 20-pound bird already yields 9 pounds of cooked meat, which is 144 ounces and 9 ounces for each of 16 people.

Should I cook a second turkey if more people are coming?

No, and the reason is the oven, not the freezer. A second bird or a bone-in breast needs two to four and a half hours at 325 degrees, which is the same cavity your first turkey occupies from eleven in the morning until three-thirty. If you want extra meat at noon, buy a spiral-cut ham, three rotisserie chickens, or four pounds of chicken thighs that can go on a sheet pan after the bird comes out.

Can a 20-pound turkey feed 16 people?

Yes, at 9 ounces of cooked meat each. Dressed weight yields about 45 percent cooked meat, so 20 pounds gives 9 pounds, or 144 ounces, and 144 divided by 16 is 9. That is a restaurant portion rather than a steakhouse one, and it stops working the moment two people ask for seconds, which is why the sides and the gravy matter more than a second bird.

What can I add at the last minute with no shopping?

Bread, gravy, and a board. A loaf is three cups of flour and ninety minutes with commercial yeast, one and a third cups of extra gravy costs three tablespoons each of fat and flour, and a plate of cheese, apples, pickles, and crackers takes ten minutes and buys the table twenty. Together they add more to a plate than a pound of meat does.

The Numbers to Write Down

Three pounds of potatoes, twelve rolls, four cups of stuffing, one and a third cups of gravy, one cup of cranberry sauce, one pie. A 20-pound bird carries 16 at 9 ounces and no more, and 3 more pounds of cooked meat means 6.7 pounds raw that the oven cannot take today.

The exception is a table with children under ten. Four kids eat like two adults on meat and like four on rolls, so halve the protein and double the bread. If the count went up because of a thaw failure rather than a guest, the cold water plan is the other article.
