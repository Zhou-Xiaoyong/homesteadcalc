Hens Stopped Laying Before Thanksgiving: What Do You Do?

Published: November 23, 2026 · 8 min read

Buy two dozen eggs today and bake the pies with those. Six hens in a hard molt are giving you five to eight eggs a week, Thursday's table wants twenty-three, and nothing you hang in the coop tonight closes that gap in three days. Light and an 18 percent ration do work. On a four-week clock, which puts the payoff at Christmas.

This page counts what a twelve-person dinner burns, then sorts the three-day fixes from the eight-week ones. Most advice mixes the two and sells you a timer for a problem a twenty-dollar bill solved.

Twenty-Three Eggs for Twelve People

Thanksgiving is the one meal of the year where eggs are structure, not garnish. Count line by line.

Line on the table, 12 people Eggs What happens without them
Two 9-inch pumpkin pies 4 Filling never sets. It bakes to sweetened squash soup
One pecan pie 3 Corn syrup and butter stay liquid under the nuts
36 dinner rolls 2 One in the dough, one for the wash. Skip both and they bake dense and pale
Cornbread stuffing, 13-by-9 pan 2 Loose crumbs instead of a pan you can cut
Deviled eggs, 24 halves 12 Nothing. The dish is the eggs
Total 23 11 with the deviled eggs cut

Twelve of those twenty-three sit on one plate, which is why the appetizer leaves the menu first. The pumpkin pie math runs 2 eggs per custard whatever the squash cost, the roll batch is a 36-roll formula built on 2, and a 15-cup pan of stuffing sets on 2 as well.

Eleven is a different animal from twenty-three. A flock half in molt can find eleven in ten days. It cannot find twenty-three by Thursday, and no arrangement of nest boxes changes that.

What Six Hens Are Actually Giving You

Read the flock before you shop. Molt runs head to tail, primaries last, and the stage of that sequence says how many weeks are left.

Bird What you see Weeks left Eggs this week
Hen 1, hard molt Bare neck, pinfeathers just breaking 8 to 10 0
Hen 2, hard molt Naked back, tail gone 6 to 8 0
Hen 3, light molt Primaries half grown 3 to 4 0 to 1
Hen 4, light molt Primaries nearly full 1 to 2 1 to 2
Hens 5 and 6, done molting Full coat, short daylight None 2 to 3 each
Flock of six 5 to 8

Primaries half regrown means about week six, and lay restarts one to three weeks after the last one hardens. A hen with pinfeathers on her bare neck is at week one of eight. She is not helping this month.

Daylight does the rest. At 40 degrees north on November 23 the sun is up nine hours and forty minutes, and hens want fourteen to sixteen to lay at full rate. The flock-size math averages four eggs per hen per week across a year, molting and winter included, and late November sits well under that. Six hens can look healthy and hand you six eggs.

The Three-Day Column and the Four-Week Column

Light, on a morning timer. Five hours before dawn takes a 9 hour 40 minute day to 14 hours 40. Set the timer for 4:30 a.m. and let the sun finish it. Do not stretch the evening instead: hens need dusk to find the roost.

The electricity is the cheapest part. A 9-watt LED for five extra hours draws 45 watt-hours a day, 1.35 kilowatt-hours a month, about 23 cents at 17 cents a kilowatt-hour. The old 60-watt bulb in the same socket burns 300 watt-hours a day and costs $1.53 a month.

Feed at 18 percent. Feathers run about 85 percent protein by dry weight, so a hen rebuilding a coat is running a construction project. Sixteen percent layer pellets are maintenance. An 18 percent ration, or a 20 percent flock raiser, for the six to eight weeks the coat takes. Six hens at a quarter pound a day eat 1.5 pounds, so a 50-pound bag runs 33 days.

Skip the calcium. Oyster shell fixes thin shells in a laying hen. A bird that is not laying has no use for it.

Do not buy pullets. Started birds need two to four weeks of quarantine, pause another two or three after a move, and the point-of-lay birds on offer in late November are usually a spring hatch heading into its own first molt. That is $25 a bird for eggs in January.

Substitutes, and the Two Pies That Refuse Them

Egg replacements are sold as a general answer and are not one. Each works in one job and fails outside it.

Replacement Per egg Works in Fails in
Ground flax gel 1 tbsp flax plus 3 tbsp water Cookies, muffins, meatloaf, pancakes Custard, meringue, anything that must set
Aquafaba 3 tbsp chickpea liquid Whipped toppings, binding, vegan mayo Pumpkin and pecan filling
Commercial egg replacer Per the box, usually 1 1/2 tsp plus 2 tbsp water Cakes, quick breads, boxed mixes Fillings, glazes, egg wash
Applesauce 1/4 cup Moisture in quick breads and cakes Anything where the egg is the structure

Two dishes on Thursday's table have no substitute. Pumpkin and pecan filling set when egg proteins coagulate between 175 and 185 degrees Fahrenheit, so take the eggs out and the oven has nothing to firm up. The pie goes in liquid and comes out liquid with a skin. Four eggs left in the house belong in the pie tins, not the cookies.

Rolls and stuffing forgive more than they look. Dough without egg still rises and bakes, it just browns less and stales a day sooner, and a milk wash does most of what the egg wash did. Stuffing holds on stock and butter alone, though it crumbles at the cut.

Buying Eggs the Week of Thanksgiving

Retail egg prices climb into the holiday because every kitchen in the county bakes at once. Expect $4 to $6 a dozen for large grade A. Two dozen covers the menu with one spare, and the spare is the one that cracks on the way home.

Storage is where a home flock wins. Unwashed eggs hold three to five weeks at 40 degrees Fahrenheit or below, because the bloom on the shell slows moisture loss and blocks bacteria. Washing strips it. Keep the carton on a shelf, not the door. Store eggs are washed already, so they keep closer to three weeks.

If Thursday Morning Arrives Short

Work the menu in order of what the eggs are doing. Deviled eggs go first, all twelve, because the dish is nothing but the ingredient. Then cut the egg wash for a milk wash. Thirteen eggs recovered and a table that still looks complete.

The pies are not negotiable. A pie without eggs wastes an hour and a squash, and one made properly beats two that puddle. Then set the timer for 4:30 a.m. and put the 18 percent feed in the trough. Hens nearly through their molt are back on the nest by the second week of December, the bare-backed ones by New Year, and a flock on a 14-hour day lays through January instead of taking it off.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will a coop light make molting hens lay again this week?

No. A hen rebuilding a full coat has shut down the ovary on purpose, and no light overrides that. Hens that have finished molting respond to a 14 to 16 hour day in two to four weeks. With Thanksgiving three days out, the light is a Christmas investment, not a Thursday fix.

How many eggs does Thanksgiving dinner for twelve need?

Twenty-three for a table with two pumpkin pies, one pecan pie, 36 rolls, a 13-by-9 pan of stuffing, and 24 deviled egg halves. The pies take 7, the rolls 2, the stuffing 2, and the deviled eggs 12. Drop the deviled eggs and the same dinner needs eleven.

Should you wash fresh eggs before storing them?

No. A hen puts a bloom on the shell that slows moisture loss and blocks bacteria, and washing strips it. Store unwashed eggs in the carton at 40 degrees Fahrenheit or below and they hold three to five weeks. Rinse them under warm water right before you crack them, not before you put them away.

Can you substitute flax or applesauce for eggs in pumpkin pie?

Not in the pie. Pumpkin and pecan filling set when egg proteins coagulate between 175 and 185 degrees Fahrenheit, so removing the eggs leaves sweetened squash that never firms. Flax gel, aquafaba, and applesauce work in cookies, muffins, and quick breads, where something else provides the structure.

The Numbers to Write Down

Twenty-three eggs feed twelve people, eleven without the deviled plate. Six hens in hard molt give five to eight a week, and a hen needs 14 to 16 hours of light and two to four weeks to answer it, so this molt costs you Thanksgiving and repays you at Christmas. Eighteen percent feed for the length of the coat, 50 pounds per 33 days for six birds, unwashed eggs at 40 degrees for three to five weeks.

The exception is a flock half in molt. Two hens laying at winter speed and two finishing up will hand you eleven eggs inside ten days, which covers everything but the appetizer. Then the list is one dozen and the plate is a cheese board.
