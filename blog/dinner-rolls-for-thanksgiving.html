How Many Dinner Rolls for Thanksgiving?

Published: November 9, 2026 · 8 min read

Thirty-six rolls feeds twelve people at Thanksgiving. That is two and a half a guest, rounded up to three dozen, and it comes out of 5 pounds of dough, 10 cups of flour, and three 13-by-9 pans.

The roll is the only thing at the table that people take twice without appearing to decide anything. Potatoes get one scoop, stuffing gets one scoop, and the basket goes around three times. Nobody remembers the third pass, which is why the count runs generous.

Seventeen days out, the useful work is the shopping list and the freezer plan. The recipe can wait until the 25th.

Two and a Half a Guest, Then Round Up to the Dozen

Two rolls per person is the number for a Sunday dinner. Thanksgiving runs higher because the meal is long and the bird takes an hour to carve and pass. Twelve guests at two and a half is 30 rolls, and 30 is an awkward number to bake. Round to 36.

Every pan in a normal kitchen holds twelve, and dough splits by weight while a half roll does not exist. Baking 36 also puts a dozen in a bag for whoever drives home with leftovers.

Children under ten eat one roll. If the stuffing is built from your own bread, one 500-gram loaf covers a 20-pound bird, and the garden share of a twelve-person dinner works out what that costs in flour and ground.

What a Roll Actually Weighs

A standard dinner roll is two ounces of dough, 57 grams, and it proofs to about 2 3/4 inches across. Bakery rolls sold by the dozen often run three to four ounces, and a Parker House roll is closer to 1 3/4 ounces because it is flattened and folded before the second rise.

The batch at the bottom of this page weighs 4.9 pounds once the milk, butter, sugar, and egg are in. Divide that by 36 and each piece is 62 grams, a hair over two ounces.

The Pan Decides How Many You Can Bake

Rolls are a packing problem. A 13-by-9 pan is 117 square inches. Twelve rolls get 9.75 square inches each, which is a 3.1-inch square, and a proofed roll measures about 2 3/4 inches. That gap is why twelve come out separate, with crust on four sides.

Fifteen get 7.8 square inches each. They touch in the proof, fuse in the oven, and tear apart at the table. Both are defensible; they are different breads.

Pan Area Rolls that fit Square inches each Result

13 by 9, five rows of three 117 sq in 15 7.8 Pull-apart, soft sides
Half sheet, 18 by 13 234 sq in 24 9.75 Two pans in one
10-inch round 78.5 sq in 7 11.2 One center, six around

A half sheet holds exactly two pans' worth, so 36 rolls is one half sheet plus one 13-by-9. Two racks and one oven means the third pan waits, which matters ten minutes before dinner.

Ten Cups of Flour, Three and a Quarter Cups of Liquid

The batch holds at 65 percent hydration, 1.8 percent salt, and 2 percent instant yeast, which are the calculator's own figures for rolls. This makes 36 rolls at 62 grams.

Weigh the milk rather than pouring by eye. A dough 5 percent wetter spreads in the pan and the rolls merge whether you wanted them to or not.

Mix Wednesday, Bake at Eleven

Thanksgiving morning has no free hour in it. Wednesday night does.

Mix at eight, let the dough bulk for an hour on the counter, punch it down, and put the covered bowl in the fridge. Thursday at eight, weigh and shape 36 pieces at 62 grams each, set them in buttered pans, and let them proof at room temperature for 1 1/2 to 2 hours. They go in at 375 degrees Fahrenheit for 15 to 18 minutes, until the tops are mahogany and the middle reads 190 to 200 degrees on an instant-read thermometer.

The oven is the real constraint. A 20-pound bird comes out at 325 and rests for 30 minutes, and the rolls need 18. That rest is the roll window: the bake time belongs on the schedule next to the turkey, not after it. The freezer math for the bird covers the space; this covers the 18 minutes it sits on the counter.

Twenty seconds too long in a microwave turns the crust to leather. Wrapped in foil at 350 for 8 minutes brings it back.

The Rolls Take Fifty Square Feet of Wheat

Here is the part that surprises people who grow their own. Those 10 cups of flour weigh 2.66 pounds. Wheat to flour runs about 1.1 to 1, so the pan needs close to 2.9 pounds of wheat. A small plot yields 50 to 60 pounds per 1,000 square feet, which puts the roll pan at about 53 square feet, a bed 4 feet wide and 13 feet long.

Potatoes return 1.5 pounds per foot of row, near 500 pounds per 1,000 square feet. Wheat returns 55. Same ground, nine times the food, which is why almost nobody grows flour for the bread basket. The Garden Yield Planner will run both crops side by side if you want to see the gap on your own beds.

Buy the flour. Grow the potatoes, the squash, and the herbs, which is what the potato math and the pumpkin math both land on. Then bake 36 rolls that cost 12 cents each.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many dinner rolls per person for Thanksgiving?

Two rolls a person at a normal dinner and two and a half at Thanksgiving, because the basket goes around three times. Twelve guests need 30 rolls, and you bake 36 because every pan holds a dozen and dough divides more easily than it halves. Children under ten eat one roll each.

How many rolls fit in a 13x9 pan?

Twelve rolls fit with a small gap between them, in four rows of three. Crowd fifteen in and they proof into each other, which gives you pull-apart rolls with soft sides instead of separate ones with crust all around. A half sheet pan holds 24, exactly two 13-by-9 pans.

How much dough do I need for 36 dinner rolls?

Just under five pounds. The milk, butter, sugar, and egg push the dough past the flour-and-water figure, so weigh it and divide by 36 for pieces of 62 grams. The batch takes 10 cups of flour, 3 1/4 cups of milk, 22 grams of salt, and 24 grams of instant yeast.

Can I make dinner rolls ahead and freeze them?

Yes, and the freezer is the right place for them. Bake Wednesday, cool completely, and freeze in bags. Reheat wrapped in foil at 350 degrees Fahrenheit for 12 to 15 minutes. Skip the refrigerator: bread stales fastest just above freezing, so two days in the fridge does more damage than two weeks in the freezer.

The Numbers to Write Down

Two and a half rolls a guest, 62 grams a piece, twelve to a 13-by-9 pan, 10 cups of flour per 36, 375 degrees for 15 to 18 minutes, and 53 square feet of wheat if you insist on growing it. The rolls come out of a bird's 30-minute rest, and they freeze better than they refrigerate.

The exception is a family that eats rolls as the meal. For that table, double to 72, bake four pans, and freeze two on Wednesday so Friday's sandwiches have bread.

For the rest of the menu, the garden share of a twelve-person dinner and the freezer plan cover the other three quarters of the plate. If you keep a starter, the feeding ratios explain how to put that culture into this dough instead of commercial yeast.
