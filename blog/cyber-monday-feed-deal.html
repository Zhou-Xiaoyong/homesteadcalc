Cyber Monday Feed Deal: How Many Bags Are Worth Buying?

Published: November 28, 2026 · 8 min read

Four bags, for six hens. That is 200 pounds, 111 days at winter intake, and about the ceiling on what a Cyber Monday feed deal is worth. Twenty percent off a year's supply is not a saving, because the vitamins fade faster than the discount compounds.

The limit is not the percentage on the banner. It is how many weeks the feed stays worth feeding, which is 90 days for most backyard flocks. What follows is that arithmetic, plus the shipping rule that decides whether the fourth bag even arrives.

Why the Ceiling Is 90 Days and Not a Year

Mills stamp a mill date on the bag seam and suggest using the contents within 8 to 12 weeks. Vitamin A, D3, and E are the fragile part: they go in as a premix and lose potency from the day of milling, faster in heat. The corn and soybean meal underneath holds its calories far longer than the label is worth.

Fat is the second clock. Layer rations carry 3 to 5 percent added fat, and it oxidizes into the smell of old oil. Hens cut intake before you notice anything else, and a hen eating 10 percent less in January lays fewer eggs in February.

The Winter Bump Changes the Bag Count

The 0.25 pound per hen per day that extension guides use is a laying-season number. Below freezing, a bird holding a body temperature near 105°F burns more calories and intake climbs 15 to 25 percent. Plan winter at 0.30.

Six hens at 1.5 pounds a day eat 45 pounds a month in June and 54 in January. December through February is 162 pounds, not the 135 you get by running the summer rate across the same calendar.

What the Discount Is Actually Worth

At $19 for 50 pounds, layer feed is 38 cents a pound. Twenty percent off is 7.6 cents a pound, $3.80 a bag, $15.20 on four. That is the whole deal.

Now price the alternative. A hanging feeder at back height, filled two-thirds, keeps 10 to 15 percent of the ration out of the litter. On six hens at 1.8 pounds a day that is 99 pounds a year, close to $37, and it pays again every winter after.

Feed also has a floor no Monday sale moves. It tracks corn and soybean meal, and a co-op selling cash-and-carry often sits a few dollars a bag under the farm-store shelf. Ask for the price per ton before you check out. If the mill is already cheaper, the sale is decoration.

The Fourth Bag Goes Freight

Both major parcel carriers cap a package at 150 pounds. Three bags ride as a parcel. The fourth turns the order into freight, quoted by the pallet, with a liftgate fee if you live on gravel.

Free-shipping thresholds are written against merchandise value, not weight, and the weight exception lives in the fine print. Ask one question before paying: parcel or freight, and what delivery window? Freight from the Midwest to the East Coast runs five to nine business days, which is fine in November and not fine if the can is empty.

Practical version: three bags online, the fourth from the farm store in the truck. You lose $3.80 and gain feed this week.

Where Four Bags Actually Go

A 50-pound bag takes about 1.5 cubic feet, so four bags is 6. A 32-gallon galvanized can holds roughly 100 pounds, or two bags, which makes the answer two cans and a pallet.

Keep them off the concrete. It wicks ground moisture into the bottom bag, and feed at 14 percent moisture molds; clumps are the visible sign, aflatoxin the invisible one. Chickens are among the more sensitive livestock to it, and mixing moldy feed into clean feed does not fix it.

Rodents need a quarter inch of gap. A plastic tote with a snap lid is a suggestion. Stack no more than five high, or the bottom bag splits at the seam.

If the Feed Price Is Thin, Buy This Instead

Two purchases beat a feed discount on a small flock, and neither spoils: rodent-proof storage, and a feeder that stops the spill. A deep dish on the floor wastes enough that the bag counts above can be off by a fifth; a trough at back height brings the loss back toward 10 percent.

Neither goes on sale in a way that matters. Buy them Tuesday at full price and you are still ahead of the person who saved $15 and lost 25 pounds of feed to the litter.

If You Missed the Sale

Feed does not get more expensive on Tuesday. Cyber Monday is a marketing window, not a price floor, and a bag bought December 3 costs what it cost November 30 unless corn moved.

Three moves instead. Ask the mill for today's price per ton and the mill date on the pallet. Buy from the back of the pallet, where the newest bags sit, since stores pull from the front. Check whether the co-op sells a one-ton tote, which is the right format past about 25 birds.

What you do keep is the timing. Intake peaks in the coldest weeks, so buy once and buy enough to still have feed in late January. That is the same 90-day window, and the reason the table rounds up.

If Twenty Bags Are Already on the Way

Twenty bags is 1,000 pounds, which for six hens at 1.8 pounds a day is 555 days of feed. A year and a half of ration for a flock that will finish it in the summer of 2028, with the vitamins gone long before the bags are.

Use the oldest first and write the mill date on the can lid, because every bag looks alike by the second month. Keep the pile under 70°F if you can; a garage that hits 95 in July roughly doubles the rate of loss. Split a pallet with a neighbor who keeps hens.

Trust the smell. Fresh feed smells sweet and grainy; sour, sharp, or old oil means rancid fat, and clumps mean moisture. Either one goes to the compost. Losing $19 on a bad bag beats a flock that stops laying for three weeks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is chicken feed actually cheaper on Cyber Monday?

Usually 15 to 20 percent off the shelf price, which on a 19-dollar bag is 3 dollars and 80 cents. That is real money on four bags and 15 dollars total for six hens. It stops being real when the order is big enough to ship as freight, because the freight quote eats the discount before the feed reaches the barn.

How many bags of chicken feed should I buy at once?

Ninety days of feed, rounded up to whole bags. Six hens eat 1.8 pounds a day in winter, so 90 days is 162 pounds and four 50-pound bags. Three hens need two bags, twelve need seven, and twenty-five need fourteen. Buying past that point trades a discount you can measure for vitamin loss and rodent pressure you cannot.

How long does chicken feed last in storage?

Eight to twelve weeks is the working window for a crumbled or mashed layer ration, and up to about four months for pellets kept cool and dry. The vitamins go before the calories do. Vitamin A and D3 in the premix lose potency steadily after milling, faster in heat, while the corn and soybean meal underneath stays nutritionally sound for much longer than the label is worth.

Can I store feed in a galvanized trash can?

Yes, and a 32-gallon can holds about 100 pounds, or two bags. Set it on a pallet instead of the concrete floor, keep the lid weighted, and check the inside of the lid in January. A cold can carried into a warm barn sweats on the inside, and that condensation is the only way feed molds in a sealed can.

The Numbers to Write Down

Four bags for six hens, 200 pounds, 111 days at 1.8 pounds a day, $15.20 saved at 20 percent off a $19 bag. Two metal cans on a pallet to hold it. Three bags if you are shipping, four if you are driving.

The exception is a flock past 25 birds with a bulk tote available, where a ton priced today beats any percentage off a bag. Below that, the discount is not the limit. The ninety days a bag stays worth feeding is, which makes the fourth bag the last sensible one and the twentieth a year and a half of fading vitamins in a hot garage.

When cold is the real deadline rather than the sale, water comes first: a hen eating 1.8 pounds a day drinks close to a pint, and an iced bucket suppresses intake faster than any feed decision.
